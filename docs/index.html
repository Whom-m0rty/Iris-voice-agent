A voice that uses your computer for you.

Say what you want, in plain words. Iris hears it through AssemblyAI, does it in any app on your Windows PC, tells you what happened, and asks before anything that can't be undone. Made for people who can't see the screen, and for anyone the screen confuses.

Screen readers stop where apps stop labelling their buttons.

A screen reader reads the accessibility tree. When an app draws its own icons, all you hear is “button, button, button”. Iris reads the tree when it is there and looks at the pixels when it isn't. You don't learn shortcuts. You say what you want.

How it works

The fast part does almost all the work. The slow part steps in only when it has to. The colours are the ones Iris draws on your screen, so a sighted helper can see who is acting.

  1. You speakAny English. Pauses mid-sentence are fine.
  2. AssemblyAI hearsStreaming speech-to-text.
  3. Claude plansWhat to say and which tool to use.
  4. An API, or the screenGmail and web pages directly. Otherwise Kev (~230 ms) and Claude vision click in the app.
  5. Asks firstAnything that sends, pays or deletes waits for your spoken yes.

Ears: AssemblyAI

Universal-Streaming speech-to-text. Short pauses are merged into one turn, because people, older people especially, pause mid-sentence. A second backend runs the whole voice loop on AssemblyAI's Voice Agent API.

Brain: Claude

Turns what you said into small steps and picks the tool: an API when one exists, the screen when it doesn't. Results are read from the screen, never guessed.

Hands: Kev, then vision

Kev, an open-weights decision model on your GPU, picks each button from the accessibility tree in about 230 ms. When the tree has no answer, Claude looks at the pixels, snaps to the real control, or zooms in for a second look.

Guard: your spoken yes

Anything that sends, pays, deletes or shares waits for a yes, judged on your exact words. “Uh… who is it for?” gets the question again, not an action.

Install on your PC

Iris is a Windows program, not a website: it works on your screen, in your apps. Setup takes about 15 minutes, most of it a one-time download of the local model.

You need Windows 10 or 11, an NVIDIA GPU with about 10 GB free (for Kev), a microphone and headphones, a free AssemblyAI key, and Claude (a Claude Code login or an Anthropic API key).

Who sets it up Someone comfortable with a terminal, once. After that, the person using Iris only needs their voice.

  1. Get the code. 
    git clone https://github.com/Whom-m0rty/Iris-voice-agent
cd Iris-voice-agent
  2. Install. This downloads Kev and the Python packages, then asks for your keys. 
    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File install.ps1
  3. Optional: connect Gmail, and list apps Iris must never touch in protected_apps.txt. 
    python voice\mcp_servers\mail.py login
  4. Start it, put on headphones, and talk. 
    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File run_demo.ps1
    Try “What's the newest file in my Downloads folder?” or “Find a video on how to bake bread and play it.”

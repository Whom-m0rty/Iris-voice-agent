Iris.The solution04 / 13

Say it. Iris does it, and asks first.

You say what you want in plain words. Iris does it in any app on your PC, tells you what happened, and waits for your spoken yes before anything that sends, pays or deletes.

  1. YouPut vitamin D in my basket.
  2. IrisDone. One bottle of vitamin D3 is in your basket.
  3. YouNow check out.
  4. IrisCheck out and pay for your basket? It costs €32.86.
  5. YouNo, wait.
  6. IrisOkay. I didn't buy anything.

A real run on amazon.it, retold in short.

Each model does what it's best at.

ears

AssemblyAI

Universal-Streaming turns speech into text as you talk.

streaming
brain

Claude

One JSON decision per turn: what to say, which tool.

~2.5 s per turn
hands

Kev, local

Open-weights decision model picks each control from the tree.

234 ms
eyes

Claude vision

Only when the tree is empty: looks, then snaps to the real control.

3.2 s
guard

Your yes

Word list + Kev decide what is risky; your exact words decide yes.

129 ms

The fast part does almost all the work. The slow part steps in only when it has to. Kev, vision and yes/no latencies: bench/bench_full.py on an RTX 5060 Ti. Brain turn time seen in live runs.

Iris.Why you can trust it09 / 13

It never acts on a maybe.

0false yeses in 60 spoken answers
0risky actions let through, in 40 it had never seen
92%controls picked right by Kev in 25 test steps; misses went to vision, not to a wrong click
10/10unlabelled icons found by Claude vision

Passwords never reach a model: code types them from Windows Credential Manager. Apps on the do-not-touch list are never read or clicked. Muted, a pending question can only end as a no.

Measured with bench/bench_full.py on our own test sets, 26.09.2026.

Iris.Made for people who can't see the screen10 / 13

Small behaviours that change the day.

“What's on my screen?”

Where you are, pop-ups first, three things that matter. Photos described by vision.

“I'm lost.”

Says where you are and what box is open. Never closes it without asking.

“Show me how.”

Does the task slowly, explaining each step in plain words. It teaches as it goes.

“Say that again.”

Repeats the last reply word for word, at once.

“Wait longer for me.”

Pause length set by voice, remembered next time.

tick · chime · low tone

Sounds for thinking, done and error. Silence never leaves you guessing.
